# Photographer Workflow Research: Collections, Culling and Rating

Prepared 2026-09-25 for the Lightroom Classic replacement spec.

**About the evidence.** Nobody has published a proper survey of how photographers use collections or flags, stars and labels. What follows comes from forum threads (Lightroom Queen, Adobe Community, DPReview, BCG), tutorials by recognised educators (Bampton, Kelby, Kloskowski/Sylvan, Krogh, Rung), vendor documentation and reviews. Reddit threads did not show up in search indexing. Where a claim is my own inference, it is marked **[inference]**. Numbers published by vendors (Aftershoot, Imagen, FilterPixel) are marketing and are marked as such.

---

## Question A: Collections

### A1. What the Lightroom Classic features are, and what people use them for

| Feature | What it is | Typical real use |
|---|---|---|
| Collection | A manual list of photos, like a playlist. A photo can sit in many collections. | Client selects, portfolio, book, slideshow and print sets, competition entries |
| Collection set | A container for collections, with no photos of its own | One set per shoot or client (Kelby's "Full Shoot / Picks / Selects"), one set per year, or sets named "Portfolio" and "Projects" |
| Smart collection | A saved rule-based search. Alt/Option-click on "+" gives nested any/all/none rules. | "5 stars", "Not yet edited", "keyword X and Y", "lens = 100mm macro", "Picks this year", "no keywords" |
| Quick Collection / target collection | A temporary basket, or any collection set as target. Press **B** to add. | Gathering photos from many folders for a one-off export or print; building one chapter at a time |
| Publish collection | A collection tied to a destination (hard drive, Flickr, SmugMug, Adobe Stock, WordPress plugins) that tracks what has changed and needs republishing | Keeping a website, SmugMug or Flickr gallery, TV/Dropbox folder or stock portfolio in sync |
| Synced collection | A collection that syncs to Lightroom cloud as an album (smart previews only) | Mobile and iPad portfolios, client review in the web gallery, editing on the go |

Sources: [Adobe Community: folders vs collections](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic-discussions/difference-between-folders-and-collections/td-p/9625983); [Lightroom Queen: smart collections](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/use-smart-collection/); [Photofocus: Quick Collection](https://photofocus.com/software/what-is-lightrooms-quick-collection/); [Matt K: target collections](https://mattk.com/target-collections-the-hidden-organizing-feature/); [Julieanne Kost: collections](https://jkost.com/blog/2024/06/organizing-photos-using-collections-in-lightroom-classic.html); [nested smart rules, DPS](https://digital-photography-school.com/using-smart-collections-lightroom/)

### A2. Use by photographer type

- **Wedding and event.** Folders per job are dominant, and often the whole organising system. A wedding is one shoot, so a dated folder with subfolders ("Getting Ready", "Ceremony") covers most needs. In a Lightroom Queen thread, a wedding pro since 2011 asks "I don't use Collections, tell me why I should." Responders agree that wedding shooters are the exception for whom folders are "perfectly fine". They suggest collections only for cross-wedding portfolios and album or blog picks. ([LRQ thread](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/i-dont-use-collections-tell-me-why-i-should.34620/)) Some wedding tutorials recommend a per-client collection set ("Client – Wedding All", then selects and parts of the day) ([sproul.photography](https://www.sproul.photography/post/lightroom-classic-collections-vs-smart-collections-when-to-use-each-for-optimal-photo-organisation), [Richard Photo Lab](https://richardphotolab.com/blogs/post/wedding-workflow-wonders)). Increasingly, culling happens before Lightroom in Aftershoot, Narrative or Photo Mechanic, so the "selects" collection is replaced by importing only the picks. Delivery goes to an external gallery (Pixieset, Pic-Time, ShootProof) via export or a plugin.
- **Portrait and studio.** Per-client "Full Shoot, Picks, Selects" collection sets (Kelby), a client proofing set, and synced collections to share a Lightroom web gallery for client favourites ([Scott Kelby](https://scottkelby.com/how-i-use-lightrooms-collections/)).
- **Landscape and travel.** The heaviest collection users. Uses include portfolio or "best of" collections, trip sets (Kelby's travel workflow), print and book sets ("Cuba Book"), monthly club competition entries, smart collections by keyword or location for web pages, and publish services to SmugMug, Flickr or a website ([LRQ thread](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/i-dont-use-collections-tell-me-why-i-should.34620/); [Kelby travel workflow](https://scottkelby.com/trying-out-a-new-lightroom-workflow-for-travel-photography/); [Chasing Hippoz target collections](https://www.chasinghippoz.com/travel-photo-blog/using-the-target-collection-tool-with-collection-sets-in-lightroom)).
- **Sports and news.** Largely outside Lightroom for selection. Photo Mechanic handles ingest, tagging with **T**, star ratings, code replacements for captions, and FTP or wire transmission. "Nearly all" photographers in NFL photo rooms use it. Lightroom, if used at all, is only for toning. These photographers make little use of collections ([Sideline Captions](https://sidelinecaptions.com/what-are-code-replacements-photo-mechanic); [Picture Storyteller](https://picturestoryteller.com/2013/06/14/photomechanic-is-a-must-have-for-the-editorialsports-photographer/); [Kelby sports workflow](https://scottkelby.com/my-sports-photography-workflow-so-far/)).
- **Stock and commercial.** Keywording is their core work. They use smart collections for "no keywords yet" or "ready for stock", the Adobe Stock publish service, and per-client or per-job collection sets. **[inference, based on the Adobe Stock publish service and keyword-driven smart collections in the LRQ and Adobe docs]**
- **Hobbyist, wildlife and nature.** Keyword smart collections by species or subject across many dated folders (for example, one user's scuba site built from tag-based smart collections, and wildlife by animal). Also year-in-review and "favourites" collections, and synced collections for showing on phone or iPad ([LRQ thread](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/i-dont-use-collections-tell-me-why-i-should.34620/)).

### A3. The most common concrete use cases, roughly ranked [inference from source frequency]

1. **Output-oriented selections:** portfolio or best-of, print sets, book or slideshow sources, competition entries, blog and social queues. This is the canonical use: Krogh's "Store (folders), Tag (keywords), Create (collections)" ([The DAM Book](https://thedambook.com/folders-keywords-or-collections/)).
2. **Per-shoot selection tiers:** Full Shoot, Picks, Selects (Kelby), or client proofing sets.
3. **Workflow smart collections:** 5 stars, picks, "not yet edited" (Has Adjustments = false), "no keywords", "edited in Photoshop", and color-label status ([LRQ smart collection example](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/use-smart-collection/)).
4. **Publishing:** SmugMug, Flickr, hard-drive or Dropbox folders for a TV or family, and websites via plugins. The users who rely on it find it "essential", but even a Lightroom Killer Tips author stopped using it because it is "locked into the catalog" ([LKT: Do you use publish services?](https://lightroomkillertips.com/do-you-use-publish-services/)).
5. **Mobile and cloud sync:** portfolios on iPad or phone, and web sharing ([LRQ sync limitations](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/limitations-syncing-classic-with-cloud/)).
6. **Temporary baskets:** Quick Collection or target collection with **B** for gathering across folders ([Photofocus](https://photofocus.com/photography/using-target-collections-in-lightroom/)).

### A4. How many people ignore collections?

No numbers exist. What the sources do show:
- Recurring "folders vs collections" threads are among the most common beginner confusions on Adobe Community and LRQ (for example [1](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic-discussions/confusion-over-organization-of-images-in-lightroom/m-p/13356184), [2](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/help-with-folders-vs-collections.28229/), [3](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/using-collections-instead-of-folders-questions.24392/)).
- Experienced users openly say they never use collections and get by with folders, filters and keywords ("If you're happy, stick with what you're doing"; "keywording alone suffices") ([LRQ](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/i-dont-use-collections-tell-me-why-i-should.34620/)).
- Another pattern is folders for everything until export, with a single publish collection at the end ([LRQ "Using collections"](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/using-collections.45221/)).
- **[inference]** A large share of users, plausibly the majority of wedding, event and sports shooters, work folder-first and touch collections only for output. Collection power users are mostly landscape, travel, wildlife, hobbyist and stock photographers with multi-year archives.

### A5. Common complaints

- **Confusion between folders and collections.** Users don't grasp the physical versus virtual distinction, and pressing Delete inside a collection silently removes from the collection only, with no warning ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic-discussions/how-to-delete-a-copy-from-within-a-collection/m-p/12078244); ["picture deleted due to remove from collection"](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic-discussions/picture-deleted-due-to-remove-from-this-collection/td-p/11097064)).
- **Virtual copies.** They exist only in the catalog. Removing one from a collection leaves it orphaned in the folder, and users lose track of which copy lives where ([Adobe Community: virtual copies disappeared](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic-discussions/all-my-virtual-copies-disappeared/td-p/11962193); [LRQ](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/virtual-copies.47767/)).
- **Sync limits.** Smart collections don't sync. Collection sets sync only in a limited way (cloud album folders don't come back as sets). Keywords added on mobile don't return. Only one catalog can sync. Only smart previews upload. Collections sometimes empty themselves on resync. Adobe has said Classic will get no new sync features ([LRQ limitations](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/limitations-syncing-classic-with-cloud/); [LRQ "Using collections"](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/using-collections.45221/); [LRQ collection empties on sync](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/cannot-sync-collection-from-lightroom-classic-to-lightroom-without-emptying-collection.51906/); [Adobe idea: sync smart collections, 8+ pages](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic-ideas/p-ability-to-sync-smart-collections-with-ecosystem-clients/idi-p/12249033/page/8)).
- **Smart-rule expressiveness.** Nested rules exist but are hidden behind Alt/Option-click, so most users don't know about them. There are requests for more filter fields ([LRQ boolean/nested](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/boolen-and-or-adjustments-in-smart-collections-and-nested-smart-collections.10309/); [Adobe request: more advanced filters](https://community.adobe.com/questions-675/enable-more-advanced-filters-in-smart-collections-1556415)).
- **Publish services are catalog-locked**, and several third-party publish plugins have disappeared ([LKT](https://lightroomkillertips.com/do-you-use-publish-services/)).
- **No per-collection edits.** The same photo can't carry a different crop or look in two collections without a virtual copy. Capture One has the same limit (variants). **[inference; common rationale for virtual copies]**
- **Can't see collections in the Develop module or in the folder list** ([Adobe idea: show collection list in folder list](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic-ideas/p-show-collection-list-in-folder-list/idc-p/14214322)).

### A7. Recommendation for collections

**Yes, build it, but as a single small concept.** Collections are the only place multi-folder output and selection work happens (portfolio, print, book, client selects, publish), and landscape, travel and stock users depend on them. They are also a common source of confusion, and most event and sports shooters barely touch them. So they should cost nothing to ignore.

A minimal design that should cover about 90% of real use:
1. **Album:** an ordered manual list of references, with custom sort order for books and slideshows. It can hold virtual copies, which are shown as "versions".
2. **Smart album:** a saved filter using the same filter bar as the library, one level of any/all plus a "none of" group, and a visible "Save filter as smart album" button so rules aren't hidden behind a modifier key. Optional scope: a folder or album group.
3. **Album group:** a nestable folder for albums, whose optional scope applies to the smart albums inside it (Capture One's Project idea). This covers collection sets.
4. **One "Basket" (target):** a single key (B) adds to the current target album. The default target is a temporary basket. This merges Quick Collection and target collection.
5. **Destination binding (later):** any album can be linked to an export preset or destination (folder, SmugMug, web), with "modified since publish" tracking. This replaces publish collections without a separate tree.
6. **Safety:** inside an album, the Delete key says "Remove from album". Deleting a photo from disk requires an explicit command. Removing a photo from an album never orphans a virtual copy silently; the user is told if it is the copy's last membership.
7. **Portability:** store album membership in the catalog and optionally mirror it as XMP keywords or sidecar lists, so selections survive switching apps.

Skip for v1: cloud or mobile sync of albums, and per-album edit settings.

## Question B: Culling and rating

### B1. How photographers actually use flags, stars and color labels

No real survey exists; vendor claims are noted inline. These are the documented patterns, roughly by prevalence **[inference from tutorial and forum frequency]**:

1. **Flags first, then stars (the two-pass hybrid).** Reject with **X** and pick with **P**, delete the rejects, then give stars only to the picks. This is Victoria Bampton's recommendation (her own scale: 1 star for near-deletes, 3 for shareable, 5 for portfolio). It is also the most common forum answer ([LRQ Flags vs Stars](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/lightroom-ranking-flags-vs-stars-which-to-use/); [LRQ rating workflow infographic](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/rating-workflow-infographic/); [DPReview thread](https://www.dpreview.com/forums/threads/lightroom-stars-and-rejects-flags.4677978/)).
2. **Stars only, as progressive passes.** 1 star on the gut-reaction pass, 2 stars after comparing similar frames, 3 stars as final selects. Sometimes 4 stars means edited and share-worthy and 5 stars means print version (Michael Rung). One tutorial calls stars-in-first-pass "probably the most popular" way to cull, because stars are visible and portable ([Michael Rung](https://www.michaelrungphotography.com/post/pro-tips-for-using-ratings-color-labels); [The Lens Lounge](https://thelenslounge.com/how-to-cull-photos-in-lightroom/)).
3. **Flags only.** Pick or reject, filter to picks, done. Argued for as the simplest system with no interpretation problems; Lightroom even has a "Delete Rejected Photos" command ([LKT: flags over ratings](https://lightroomkillertips.com/3-and-a-half-reasons-to-use-flags-instead-of-ratings-in-lightroom/)).
4. **Color labels for workflow status, rarely for quality.** Examples: red = edit started, yellow = percolating, green = published, blue = pano/HDR/stack member (Rung); red = processed in Lightroom, green = processed in Photoshop, yellow = intermediate file (Maguire). Labels are the least common culling method ([Paul Maguire](https://paulmaguirephoto.com/2020/how-to-use-flags-stars-and-labels-together-in-lightroom/); [The Lens Lounge](https://thelenslounge.com/how-to-cull-photos-in-lightroom/); [Glen Smith](https://glensmith.co.uk/lightroom/library-rating)).
5. **Sports and news: tag and send.** In Photo Mechanic, **T** tags for transmission, stars narrow selections, and auto-advance moves on after each decision ([Mastering Lightroom PM workflow](https://mastering-lightroom.com/photo-mechanic-lightroom-workflow/); [Sideline Captions](https://sidelinecaptions.com/what-are-code-replacements-photo-mechanic)).
6. **Wedding with AI.** The AI tool assigns stars or colors per bucket (selected, highlight, maybe, blur, closed eyes, duplicate). The photographer reviews, then imports only the selects into Lightroom ([Aftershoot get started](https://support.aftershoot.com/en/articles/5223473-get-started-with-aftershoot-culling)).

"Stars for client tiers" is also seen, for example 3 stars = delivered and 5 stars = album or sneak peek. Kelby expresses the same tiers as collections: Full Shoot, Picks, Selects ([Kelby](https://scottkelby.com/how-i-use-lightrooms-collections/)).

### B2. Pain points

- **Three overlapping systems with no fixed meaning.** Every tutorial invents its own mapping. Bampton says stars suit people who can grade, and flags suit the "indecisive" ([LRQ](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/lightroom-ranking-flags-vs-stars-which-to-use/)). Star meaning also varies by folder (Maguire). A second person or app can't interpret someone else's stars.
- **Portability and XMP mapping:**
  - Stars (`xmp:Rating` 0–5) are the only universally portable signal ([Bampton](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/lightroom-ranking-flags-vs-stars-which-to-use/)).
  - Rejects: Bridge and FastRawViewer write `xmp:Rating = -1`. Lightroom ignores that and uses a flag instead ([FastRawViewer XMP](https://www.fastrawviewer.com/usermanual17/xmp-metadata); [LRQ](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/rejected-files-from-adobe-bridge.5281/)).
  - Flags were catalog-only until about LrC 13.2 (Feb 2024), which began writing flags to XMP (reported as `xmpDM:pick`/`xmpDM:good`). That comes with a bug where the flag isn't written if no other field changed. Bridge still has no pick flag ([Adobe bug](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic-bugs/p-flag-not-saved-to-xmp-if-xmp-pick-missing-from-file-metadata/td-p/14479660); [Bridge pick request](https://community.adobe.com/questions-558/please-add-pick-flag-support-in-bridge-174637)).
  - Color labels are stored as **text** (`xmp:Label`), not colors, and the match is case-sensitive. Photo Mechanic's defaults ("Winner", "Winner alt"…), Bridge's ("Select", "Second", "Approved", "Review", "To Do") and Lightroom's label sets ("Red"… or "Review Status") don't match. Capture One historically also used `photoshop:Urgency` ([Photometadata.org](https://www.photometadata.org/META-Tutorials-Adobe-Bridge-Color-Labels); [LRQ mismatch](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/mismatch-between-bridge-and-lr-classic-color-labels.41913/); [Lighting Mods](https://lightingmods.blogspot.com/2013/01/tip-color-label-matching-between-photo.html)).
  - Narrative's "tag" state isn't readable by Lightroom, and Lightroom cloud doesn't support color labels ([Narrative help](https://help.narrative.so/en/articles/7337393-lightroom-can-t-see-the-tags-or-reject-labels-i-apply-in-select); [Narrative color labels](https://help.narrative.so/en/articles/7337395-color-labels-are-not-showing-in-lightroom-classic)).
  - External XMP changes need a manual "Read Metadata from File" ([Narrative sync](https://help.narrative.so/en/articles/7337388-syncing-metadata-between-lightroom-classic-and-narrative)).
- **Slow loupe and 1:1 rendering.** Lightroom renders previews on demand, so each zoom-to-check-focus stalls. Building 1:1 previews for 1,000 raws takes about 20–45 minutes (vendor figure) ([Imagen perf tips](https://imagen-ai.com/valuable-tips/lightroom-classic-performance-tips/); [LRQ loupe lag](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/confused-about-time-lag-to-display-loupe-100.43329/)). This is the main reason Photo Mechanic, and later Narrative, exist: they read embedded JPEGs instantly ([Imagen PM vs LR](https://imagen-ai.com/valuable-tips/photo-mechanic-vs-lightroom-culling/)).
- **Comparing bursts.** Lightroom's Survey and Compare views are manual, and there is no automatic grouping of similar frames except the new Assisted Culling auto-stacks. Capture One users asked for its temporary similarity groups to become permanent stacks ([C1 community](https://support.captureone.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/10964124899869-Make-temporary-groups-created-by-similarity-in-Culling-permanent-i-e-by-implementing-a-Stacking-feature-or-allowing-to-add-Keywords-while-Culling-is-active)).
- **Checking eyes and focus** means zooming into every face, and group shots multiply that. This is the pain that face close-up panels were built to solve ([Narrative face assessments](https://narrative.so/select/face-assessments)).
- **Volume and decision fatigue.** A wedding runs 3,000–7,000 frames down to 500–1,000 delivered, and takes 3–8 hours to cull by hand (vendor figures) ([Imagen](https://imagen-ai.com/valuable-tips/best-photo-culling-app-weddings/); [Aftershoot 2025 report](https://aftershoot.com/blog/ai-in-photography-industry-report-2025/), 1,000+ AI-adopting respondents, self-selected).

**Patterns across these tools:**
1. They split **technical defects** (blur, closed eyes, misfire, exposure), which are machine-decidable, from **taste**, which the human or a learned profile decides.
2. **Group first, then pick one or more per group.** This is the core time saver.
3. **Per-face evidence shown on the image** (close-up strips, traffic lights), rather than numeric scores hidden behind hover.
4. **One key per decision, with auto-advance.**
5. **Mapping to stars and colors only at export time.**

### B4. Recommendation for selection and scoring

Replace flags, stars and labels with **one decision field, one optional grade, and machine signals**, and map them deterministically to XMP.

1. **Decision (required, tri-state): `Reject`, `Undecided` (default), `Keep`.** Keys: **X** for reject, **P** or **Space** for keep, **U** to clear. Auto-advance is on by default. This is the "flags" model users call simplest, and the one every AI tool reduces to.
2. **Grade (optional, only on Keep): 1–3**, labelled for example *Good*, *Better*, *Hero*. Keys **1**, **2**, **3**. Three levels fit what photographers actually use: near-delete or good, shareable or delivered, portfolio. Nobody uses 5 levels consistently.
3. **Workflow status is automatic, not a label.** Unedited, Edited, Exported/Published, and In album are derived from edit history and export logs. This removes the main use of color labels. Keep one freeform **"Mark"** (a single color or tag) for personal use, such as pano or HDR members.
4. **Machine signals are read-only and never overwrite the decision:** subject/eye focus (per face, with a traffic light shown on the image), eyes open, motion blur, exposure clipping, a near-duplicate/burst group ID with a suggested "best in group", and optionally an aesthetic score. The UI offers two flows. **Auto-reject defects** is a reversible one-click action, so the AI suggests and the human confirms. **Group mode** shows one group at a time, Left/Right between groups and Up/Down within, with a face close-up strip. Groups should persist as stacks.
5. **Speed prerequisite:** loupe images come from embedded JPEG or a preview cache, 1:1 is instant, and raw rendering only happens on request. This matters as much as the model.

**XMP compatibility mapping** (written on change, read on import):

| Internal | XMP written | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reject | `xmp:Rating = -1` **and** a Lightroom-style reject flag (`xmpDM:pick`/`xmpDM:good` as LrC 13.2+ writes) | Covers Bridge, FastRawViewer, Photo Mechanic and LrC |
| Keep, no grade | `xmp:Rating = 1` plus the pick flag | Bampton's portable "1 star = pick" workaround |
| Keep, grade 1/2/3 | `xmp:Rating = 2 / 3 / 5`, or a user-chosen map | 5 = hero keeps "5-star portfolio" smart filters working elsewhere |
| Undecided | `xmp:Rating = 0`, no flag | |
| Mark | `xmp:Label` with a configurable text preset (Lightroom "Red…" by default; Bridge or Photo Mechanic presets available) | Solves the case-sensitive label mismatch |
| AI signals | A private namespace (for example `ns:FocusScore`, `ns:GroupID`) plus optional hierarchical keywords (`cull|blur`, `cull|eyes-closed`) | Keywords are how Aftershoot passes filters to other apps |

On **import**, reverse the map. Stars map to Keep: 1 star becomes Keep with no grade, 2 stars grade 1, 3–4 stars grade 2, 5 stars grade 3. This is the inverse of the export map. A Rating of -1 or a reject flag becomes Reject, and labels become Mark. The mapping is shown in the UI so the user can check it.
